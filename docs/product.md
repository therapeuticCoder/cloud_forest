# Product

Cloud Forest is a calm social network for tending relationships, exchanging care, organizing cooperation, and keeping control of attention.

Its central premise is that social software should help people care for the people who matter to them without turning human attention into the product. Relationship quality matters more than quantity; not every relationship has the same meaning; cooperation matters more than engagement.

## Alpha promises

Cloud Forest 1.0-ALPHA should let a small group of trusted testers experience four things clearly:

1. **Relationship structure matters.** Party, Tribe, Guilds, and Signals represent different kinds and degrees of social meaning instead of flattening everyone into one undifferentiated graph.
2. **Care can be easy.** Asking for and providing practical aid should be possible even for people who struggle with initiation, coordination, or ordinary social maintenance.
3. **The social web can cross walls.** A Cloud Forest user should be able to receive at least one real public update from an external federated source as a Signal.
4. **The network should help, then get out of the way.** Cloud Forest does not optimize for time spent, popularity, compulsive scrolling, or helpless attention cycles.

## Product surfaces

### Curator

Curator is the ongoing relationship-cultivation workspace. Users create and tend private Characters, establish mutual Connections, and place relationships where they currently belong.

Curation is a continuing gameplay loop, not a one-time onboarding task. Relationships change. Capacity limits create meaningful choices rather than growth targets.

### Timeline

Timeline shows activity from the user's social world while preserving relational hierarchy. Party, Tribe, Guild, and Signal activity should not compete equally for attention.

Ordinary user-authored posts belong here. External Signals also belong here, but should be visually de-emphasized and collapsed by default so imported public activity does not become a miniature attention feed.

### Care

Care turns the relationship graph into practical mutual aid. The product should make requesting, offering, claiming, completing, retrying, and expressing gratitude feel simple and humane.

## Guilds in alpha

Guilds are the organized-cooperation layer: user-created groups for collaborating on care, aid, connection, projects, clubs, organizing, and other shared purposes.

For 1.0-ALPHA, Guilds are a visible promise rather than an implemented collaboration system. Testers should be able to understand that organized cooperation is coming without the project inventing membership, leadership, moderation, permissions, or group-Care systems prematurely.

## Humane-network boundaries

Cloud Forest does not use likes, repost counts, follower counts, behavioral ranking, streaks, unread-pressure mechanics, popularity scores, or infinite attention loops as product goals.

The desired feeling is: **I came here to relate and act. I did that. I can leave.**

## Canonical limits

- Party: 5 relationships
- Tribe: 100 people
- Guilds: 5 groups
- Signals: 10 sources

The user's self tile does not consume a Party slot.

These limits exist to protect attention and make curation meaningful. They are not engagement targets.
